\section{Conclusion}

We instantiated the symmetric-stigmergic schema of Parunak~\cite{parunak2025insects} in a foundation-model multi-agent system, mapping a full artifact-refinement loop onto its primitives and realizing the environment-to-agent arrow that classic asymmetric stigmergy lacks. The system places reasoning agents at the symmetric-stigmergy point of the modeling continuum, between ant-like agents and belief-desire-intention agents: each actor brings open-ended reasoning to a single patch, yet the actors coordinate only through the shared field, holding no beliefs or intentions about one another. Foundation models supply the action proposals that classic stigmergy could not enumerate, and the symmetric schema supplies the objective, pressure-based criterion for combining those proposals and for escalating capability when the field stalls.

The empirical contribution is a scoped one. On meeting room scheduling across a regenerated 1350-trial grid, decentralized symmetric-stigmergic coordination outperforms hierarchical control in the regime where strict-greedy control collapses into the rejection loop, and the advantage narrows on the smallest instances where that loop does not trigger. The collapse is instructive because the baseline shares the system's model-escalation trigger and collapses anyway: the rejection loop is a selection pathology that capability escalation alone cannot cure, and escaping it takes both field-based dispatch, so progress continues where the artifact yields, and the escalation arrow, so capability rises where it does not. On the theoretical side, the one guarantee this coordination admits is that aligned local descent terminates, in productive ticks bounded by initial pressure rather than by the size of the state space.
