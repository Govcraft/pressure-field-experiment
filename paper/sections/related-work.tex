\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

This work sits at the end of a single lineage that runs from digital pheromones to language-model agents, through five stages: the digital-pheromone abstraction of classic stigmergy, its reframing as a continuous field intermediate between agents and equations, the recognition of the environment as a first-class coordination substrate, the symmetric extension that lets the environment act back on agents, and the recent body of environment-mediated coordination for foundation-model multi-agent systems. The symmetric-stigmergic schema~\cite{parunak2025insects} already unifies these stages, so the contribution sits against each in turn rather than bridging disparate traditions.

\subsection{Digital Pheromones and Stigmergic Optimization}

Stigmergy, coordination through marks left in a shared environment, was named by Grass\'{e} to explain termite construction, where deposited material attracts further deposits and complex structure emerges without any agent holding a global plan~\cite{grasse1959stigmergie}. Parunak abstracted this into engineering principles for artificial multi-agent systems, identifying deposition, aggregation, and evaporation as the operators that make pheromone coordination robust and scalable~\cite{parunak1997ant}. Dorigo and colleagues turned the same insight into ant colony optimization, in which artificial pheromone trails guide combinatorial search through positive feedback on good paths and evaporation of stale ones~\cite{dorigo1996ant,dorigo1997acs,dorigo2004acobook}; the approach extends from path-finding to telecommunications routing~\cite{bonabeau1998iata} and is grounded in the broader account of self-organization in biological systems~\cite{camazine2001selforg}. This work inherits the deposition-and-evaporation core directly: the example bank and the rejected-patch store are pheromone fields with per-tick evaporation, and the sensed pressure field is the aggregate signal agents follow. It departs from classic ant systems in two respects, both forced by the task: the action space is open-ended natural-language patches rather than enumerated moves, and the agents reason rather than react.

\subsection{Pheromone Fields Between Agents and Equations}

Treating per-location pheromone variables as a continuous field places stigmergic models on a continuum between agent-based and equation-based modeling. Parunak showed analytically that pheromone coordination is intermediate between classical agent-based models and mean-field models, and that a model's position on that continuum is set by its pheromone parameters, notably the propagation factor~\cite{parunak2011mean}. Field-based middleware made the abstraction programmable: the TOTA model distributes computational fields over a network that agents sense and follow~\cite{mamei2009tota}, and the field metaphor recurs across catalogues of biology-inspired design patterns for distributed computing~\cite{babaoglu2006patterns}. The pressure field of this paper is such a field, and its one-hop propagation operator instantiates the propagation factor identified as the continuum control~\cite{parunak2011mean}: raising $\kappa$ spreads activation further along the temporal chain. We keep propagation deliberately shallow, one hop per tick from own pressures, so that the field stays near the agent-based end of the continuum where individual patches remain the unit of action.

\subsection{The Environment as a First-Class Substrate}

A separate strand of multi-agent systems research argued that the environment, not the agents alone, carries coordination, and should be engineered as a first-class entity. The E4MAS research roadmap formalized the environment as an explicit design dimension that mediates agent access to resources and to one another~\cite{weyns2007environment}. Methodologies for engineering self-organizing systems named gradient fields as a reusable coordination pattern and catalogued the mechanisms, positive and negative feedback, randomness, and repeated local interaction, through which global order emerges from local rules~\cite{dewolf2005engineering,serugendo2005selforganisation}. The pattern has carried industrial load: stigmergic coordination drives manufacturing execution control~\cite{hadeli2004stigmergy,valckenaers2015design}, digital pheromones coordinate swarming unmanned vehicles~\cite{sauter2005pheromones}, and delegate multi-agent systems route traffic anticipatorily over a pheromone substrate~\cite{claes2011anticipatory}. This work is an instance of that pattern: agents hold no model of one another and coordinate solely by reading and writing the shared artifact, so the environment is the entire coordination channel. What the present system adds to this strand is a concrete foundation-model realization in which the environment also reallocates agent capability, which the next stage makes precise.

\subsection{Symmetric Stigmergy and the BDI Bridge}

Classic stigmergy is asymmetric: agents change the environment, but the environment does not change agent state. The symmetric extension~\cite{parunak2025insects} adds the reverse arrow, so that the environment updates agent state, and adds agent-side analogues of the environment's update, dynamics, and propagation operators. The extension lets a single schema span a continuum from ant-like agents through symmetric stigmergy to belief-desire-intention agents~\cite{bratman1987intentions,raogeorgeff1995bdi}, and it builds on a line of polyagent and hierarchical-task-network stigmergy in which reasoning-capable agents are deployed against a stigmergic substrate~\cite{brueckner2000return,brueckner2009saso}. This work instantiates the symmetric schema with foundation-model actors and classifies at the symmetric-stigmergy point on that continuum: each actor brings BDI-grade reasoning to a single patch yet holds no beliefs or intentions about the others, and the environment acts back on agents by escalating their sampling band and model tier when pressure stalls. Section~\ref{sec:symmetric-stigmergy} gives the primitive-by-primitive mapping. Where joint-intentions accounts of teamwork require each agent to hold and reason about mutual beliefs over the others' commitments~\cite{cohen1991teamwork}, symmetric-stigmergic coordination requires none: the shared field carries everything the agents need to align.

\subsection{Environment-Mediated Coordination for Foundation-Model Agents}

Most foundation-model multi-agent systems coordinate through explicit dialogue: agents pass messages, assume roles, and decompose tasks hierarchically. AutoGen formalizes conversation among configurable agents~\cite{wu2023autogen}; MetaGPT encodes standard operating procedures into role workflows over a shared message pool~\cite{hong2024metagpt}; CAMEL scripts role-play between paired agents~\cite{li2023camel}; ChatDev structures a software team as role-based chat chains over a shared codebase~\cite{qian2024chatdev}; and multi-agent debate converges instances through rounds of explicit critique~\cite{du2024debate}. These designs scale poorly: studies of agent-system scaling find that coordination overhead can turn multi-agent setups into a net cost on sequential tasks~\cite{kim2025scaling}, that most inter-agent messages are prunable without loss~\cite{zhang2025agentprune}, and that performance gains follow a saturating curve in agent count~\cite{qian2025macnet}. The surveys map the same space and note that the environment-mediated, distributed-and-cooperative quadrant remains thinly populated~\cite{guo2024llmmas_survey}.

A smaller body of work coordinates foundation-model agents through the environment rather than through messages. Generative agents organize joint activity through shared environmental co-presence and a retrievable memory stream rather than broadcast~\cite{park2023generative}; coordination benchmarks find that language-model agents perform best when coordination rests on environmental state and worst when it requires reasoning about other agents' minds~\cite{agashe2025coordination}; blackboard architectures let agents coordinate solely through a public store~\cite{han2025blackboard}; agents develop implicit numerical signaling that functions like pheromone traces even without language~\cite{buscemi2025implicit}; and information-theoretic accounts measure emergence under group-level feedback with no direct communication~\cite{riedl2025emergent}. Stigmergy proper is reaching this setting too: language-model agents have been run as pheromone-following foragers~\cite{jimenez2025swarm}, digital pheromones bridge independent reinforcement learners~\cite{xu2022stigmergic}, and decayed pheromone maps coordinate robot swarms where conventional multi-agent reinforcement learning fails~\cite{aina2025smadrl}. The norm-emergence literature shows a complementary route to implicit coordination, in which shared normative expectations rather than fields align behavior~\cite{ren2024norms,savarimuthu2024norms}, and organization-generation work automates the role structures this paper instead removes~\cite{amaral2023organisations}. Within this strand, the present system maps a full foundation-model artifact-refinement loop onto the symmetric-stigmergic primitives, including the environment-to-agent escalation arrow that the discrete blackboard and co-presence systems leave implicit. Prior work suggests but does not establish that small reasoning models struggle with purely emergent decentralized coordination~\cite{valmeekam2023planning}; Section~\ref{sec:experiments} locates the regime in which environment-mediated coordination nonetheless outperforms hierarchical control.
